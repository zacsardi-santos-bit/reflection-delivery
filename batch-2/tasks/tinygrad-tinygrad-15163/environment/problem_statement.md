I'm poking at tinygrad's symbolic simplifier and hit a gap on the modulo side. Right now the engine handles integer division nesting fine, so if you've got a scaled variable plus a small offset all divided by a larger multiple of the scale factor, it knows it can drop the low-order term and simplify. But the matching rule for modulo just isn't there. When you take that same scaled-sum-plus-bounded-offset and mod it by a larger multiple of the scale, nothing fires, even when the offset is provably smaller than the scale. This shows up constantly in index arithmetic from tensor reshapes and loop tiling, which love to generate exactly this mod pattern, so those indices aren't getting simplified as far as they should.

What I want is the modulo counterpart of the existing division rule living in the same rewrite machinery (the symbolic simplification rules in tinygrad, e.g. `@tinygrad/ops.py` where these algebraic rewrites live). Given something like a scaled variable plus a small bounded remainder, all taken mod a larger multiple of the scale, it should reduce to that bounded offset plus the variable taken mod the ratio (divisor over scale) times the scale. It's just the natural mirror of the div case, the algebra's straightforward.

Oh and once that's in, the existing recombination identity, the one that recognizes a value equals its floor-divided quotient times the divisor plus its remainder, should start firing on a broader set of inputs, since now both the div and mod sides can each get simplified independently before they're recombined back into the original. The practical payoff is that real-world image-load index expressions from actual neural net models collapse to way cleaner forms, and some validity conditions on those image loads that used to be necessary turn out to always hold, so they can drop away entirely and become unconditional accesses. Basically I want the mod side to have the same completeness the div side already does, fewer redundant bounds checks and fewer stacked modulo layers hanging around in the generated kernels.
