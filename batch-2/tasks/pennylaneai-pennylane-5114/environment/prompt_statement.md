So I've been importing wavefunctions into PennyLane from external quantum chemistry solvers, the selected-CI and DMRG kind that hand you back a state as a list of electronic configurations (determinants) with coefficients, and when I turn around and compute the energy as an expectation value of the molecular Hamiltonian I get a number that doesn't match what the solver reported. The coefficient magnitudes look fine, it's the signs that are off. I dug into it and it's a spin-ordering convention mismatch, these solvers store the spin-up and spin-down electron occupations in a different order than PennyLane's internal physics spin-orbital convention, so certain coefficients need their signs flipped when you convert between the two, and right now nobody's doing that flip so the imported state ends up with phase errors.

What I want is a utility that takes a wavefunction represented as a dictionary of Fock-basis coefficients and converts it from the chemistry spin-ordering convention to the physics spin-orbital ordering by flipping the appropriate coefficient signs. Then wire that sign correction into the state conversion routines for both of those solver formats so the returned wavefunction dicts carry the right phases. The payoff is that once a state is imported from either solver and used to compute an energy expectation value, it should match the solver's reported energy to high numerical precision. This matters because otherwise folks importing states for variational optimization or property evaluation or state prep get silently wrong energies and circuit behavior, so the goal is just making PennyLane's state faithfully represent what the chemistry solver actually produced.
