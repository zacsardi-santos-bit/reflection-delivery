I'm poking at our summarization middleware and hit a real problem with how it keeps trigger conditions in memory after init. It handles simple single-condition triggers fine (stuff like summarize after 5 messages), but it also needs to support compound triggers where multiple conditions all have to be true at once (think summarize when both token count AND message count are over their thresholds). Right now there's just one internal attribute doing double duty as both the driver of summarization logic and the thing downstream code inspects, and it assumes every condition is an independent single-metric threshold. So compound multi-condition triggers get misrepresented or silently dropped, which means anyone extending the middleware, subclassing it, or poking at its state from a debugging tool gets a wrong picture when compound triggers are in play.

What I want is two separate internal attributes after initialization. One is the canonical full representation that accurately captures every trigger variant including the compound AND clauses (the dict-form ones with multiple conditions in them). The other is a backward-compatible legacy view that only holds the simple single-condition triggers expressible as key-value pairs, so older code that used to read that internal state keeps working. The compound AND triggers should show up in the canonical one but be absent from the legacy view. Single-condition triggers, whether they come in tuple form or single-key dict form, should appear in both, each normalized to the right format for its representation. Basically canonical is the source of truth and legacy is the trimmed compatibility shim, clearly named so there's no more ambiguity about what you're looking at.
