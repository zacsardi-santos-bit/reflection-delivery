I'm reimplementing a disk usage reporting tool (basically our own `df`) and the "Avail" column is straight up wrong. It's showing the total free blocks on the filesystem, but on Linux most filesystems reserve a chunk of blocks for root, so the free count is bigger than what unprivileged users can actually write. The standard `df` that ships with Linux distros has always shown the user-available figure in its Avail column, and we should match that. Right now if a filesystem has 750 free blocks but only 600 available to normal users, our output prints 750 which is misleading, someone thinks they've got 750 worth of space and then hits a "disk full" error at 600. That's a reporting bug, not a filesystem bug.

So I want the available column to use the user-available block count instead of the total free block count. When there's reserved space and the user-available blocks are fewer than the total free blocks, the reported avail value needs to reflect what users can actually use, so in that 750 free / 600 available example it should print 600, not 750. The free/total-free figure and the available figure are genuinely different numbers on these filesystems and we're currently conflating them. Just swap the Avail column over to whatever field represents the unprivileged-available blocks. Thanks!
