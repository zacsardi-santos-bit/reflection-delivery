I'm chasing down a bug in the Dynatrace exporter for the OpenTelemetry Collector, specifically in how we serialize histogram metrics and estimate the min and max from bucket data. Right now the estimation logic walks the buckets and picks the lowest non-empty bucket boundary as the min and the highest non-empty bucket boundary as the max, which is fine most of the time but falls apart when data piles up in the overflow or underflow buckets.

Here's the thing: if all the measurements land above the highest explicit boundary, we cap the estimated max at that boundary even though the actual average (sum divided by count) is clearly higher. Same problem in reverse, if everything sits below the lowest boundary the estimated min ends up above the true average. That gives us a min/max range that doesn't contain the mean, which is mathematically impossible for a real distribution and just misleading to anyone downstream relying on these estimates for alerting or understanding their data.

So I want the estimation fixed so min is always less than or equal to the average and max is always greater than or equal to it. Concretely, when the estimated max comes out below the average, bump the max up to equal the average, and when the estimated min comes out above the average, clamp the min down to the average. That way the exported histogram always has an internally consistent min/max range relative to its mean.
