I'm building a Tower middleware that caps how many requests can be simultaneously in-flight to a wrapped service, because without a cap a slow or overwhelmed downstream just accumulates unbounded concurrent requests and you hit resource exhaustion. The idea is you hand it a maximum concurrency at construction time and once that many requests are in-flight the service reports it's not ready (via its poll_ready path) until a slot opens back up, which is basically the backpressure signal callers wait on.

The tricky part is getting capacity accounting right across every lifecycle event, so I need slots released correctly in a few scenarios. Capacity should come back when a response future completes, whether it resolves successfully or with an error. It should also come back when the response future gets dropped before it ever resolves (cancellation), and when a service clone that already reserved a slot via a readiness check gets dropped before it actually sends its request. And multiple clones of the middleware all need to share the same capacity pool so the total in-flight count across every clone stays bounded by that one configured maximum, not per-clone.

Oh and the direct-call case: if a caller skips the readiness check and calls the service anyway while it's already at capacity, the returned future should immediately resolve to an error instead of panicking or blocking. Same deal with a maximum of zero, the service should always report not-ready and any direct call just fails right away.
