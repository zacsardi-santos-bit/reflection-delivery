I'm building out the logging utilities in our infra management platform and I need a way to inspect X.509 certificate trust chains. The logging config subsystem has to look at raw certificate data and figure out trust relationships, mainly so we can reject configs that hand us self-signed certs in contexts where they aren't supported. Right now there's nothing in the logging utils package that can load certs from raw encoded byte input and check their signing relationships, so we can't tell a cert signed by a well-known authority apart from one that's self-signed.

What I want is basically two things. First, take raw encoded certificate bytes and parse them into one or more certificate objects. Second, given two of those cert objects, determine whether the first one was signed (issued) by the second, which acts as the CA. A self-signed cert checked against its own CA should come back true, a cert signed by a third-party authority checked against that same authority should come back true, and a cert checked against some unrelated authority that didn't sign it should come back false.

The reason this matters is that certain logging backends don't support self-signed certs when client auth is enabled, so being able to walk the trust chain programmatically lets us catch and reject those configs early with clear feedback instead of letting the connection blow up at runtime with cryptic errors. This whole thing is foundational for the self-signed detection we're layering on top.
