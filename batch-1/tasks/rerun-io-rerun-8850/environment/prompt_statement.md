I'm hitting a weird gap in our video decoding setup where some decoder implementations just silently produce nothing. Turns out certain codec implementations in specific environments need a bunch of extra samples queued ahead of the currently requested playback position before they'll start spitting out frames, and right now there's literally no way for a decoder to tell the player "hey I need more look-ahead than you're giving me." The player submits samples up to what's requested, the decoder sits there starved, and nobody knows why playback's broken for those files.

What I want is to add a method to the decoder abstraction (the trait/interface all the decoder implementations live behind) that lets each one declare the minimum number of additional samples it needs enqueued beyond the currently requested sample before it can produce output. Default it to zero so all the existing decoders that don't care keep working untouched, no changes needed on their end. But decoders that actually need extra look-ahead can override it and return whatever count they need.

The point is the player can then ask any decoder "how many extra samples do you need?" and adapt its queuing strategy per decoder instead of guessing. That's what makes playback smooth across the different decoder types and environments where the starved-decoder problem shows up.
