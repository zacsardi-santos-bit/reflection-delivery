I'm poking at the single-threaded executor's turn-based interface and hit two things that are bugging me. First, when I call turn there's no way to tell whether any futures actually got polled that pass. The thing I get back reports a result but nothing about work done, so I can't tell a productive turn apart from a no-op, which makes it painful to write tight polling loops or know when the executor is genuinely quiescent. I want the value returned from each turn to surface whether any futures were polled, some flag or method on that returned object, so when nothing's ready it tells me nothing was polled, and once everything's completed and the executor goes idle every turn keeps reporting nothing polled.

Second, there's a fairness bug. When one future completes and directly wakes another (say through an in-memory channel), those directly-notified futures seem to land in a separate turn from futures woken by the underlying reactor or park mechanism (like a socket going readable). So one category ends up getting priority over the other, which is bad for workloads mixing in-memory and I/O-driven futures. I want both kinds of ready futures, directly-woken and reactor-woken, picked up together in the same pass instead of spread across turns, so no category gets systematically favored or delayed.

Can you fix both? The polling status goes on whatever each turn returns, and the scheduler should pull in directly-woken and reactor-woken futures within one turn.
