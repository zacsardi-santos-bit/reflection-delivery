I'm working on the job scheduling controller and how it reacts when a job's pod specs drift away from the workload that was already created for it. A few related things are off right now.

The big one is the event message we emit when stopping a job because of a workload mismatch. Right now it's one generic message no matter what, but these are really two different situations and operators can't tell them apart. When there's an existing workload we can restore the job's pod templates from, the message should say that restoration is happening from that workload, and when there's no workload at all so restoration just can't happen, it should say that instead. Two distinct messages please.

Also the pod spec comparison logic (the bit that decides whether two sets of pod specs are equivalent) isn't strict enough. It skips tolerations entirely, so if a job's tolerations change we don't catch the mismatch. And it's got this optional flag for whether to compare pod counts, which is silly, we should just always compare counts. So make it always check both counts and tolerations, drop the optional flag.

And the state-dependent handling is wrong for one case. When a suspended job hasn't been admitted/scheduled yet and its tolerations no longer match the workload's pod specs, we shouldn't treat that as an unrecoverable error, we should just update the workload to reflect the job's current tolerations. That update path is missing today. For admitted jobs the response stays action-based on state: an admitted running job whose specs diverge gets stopped and its pod templates reverted plus the workload deleted, and a suspended-but-admitted job whose specs diverge just gets the workload deleted (no revert since it's not running).

Net effect I want: toleration changes always get detected, the right action fires based on whether the job is unadmitted-suspended vs admitted-running vs admitted-suspended, and the events actually tell operators what happened (restored from existing workload vs couldn't restore).
