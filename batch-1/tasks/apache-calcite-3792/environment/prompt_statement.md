I'm extending Apache Calcite's PostgreSQL-compatible SQL dialect and its timestamp formatting is way behind real Postgres. Right now it only handles a tiny set of format codes, so SQL that relies on PostgreSQL's date/time formatting either errors out or produces wrong output, which makes migrating apps painful. I want a formatter, invokable as a static method taking a format pattern string plus a date-time value and returning the formatted string (null result when either the pattern or the timestamp is null), that produces exactly what Postgres would.

I need it to cover 12-hour and 24-hour clock hours, minutes, seconds, seconds past midnight, milliseconds, microseconds, and fractional seconds at 1 through 6 digit precision. AM/PM indicators in uppercase, lowercase, and the dot-separated uppercase and lowercase variants. Era indicators (before/after the common era) in the same case and dot styles. Calendar year at 1 through 4 digits plus the comma-formatted four-digit form, and ISO week-year at 1 through 4 digits. Full and abbreviated month names and day-of-week names in uppercase, title case, and lowercase. Also numeric month, day-of-month, day-of-year (calendar and ISO), day-of-week both standard and ISO-numbered, week-of-month, week-of-year (calendar and ISO), century, Julian day, and quarter, oh and Roman numeral months in upper and lowercase too.

Couple of modifiers: a fill-mode prefix that suppresses leading zeros on numeric outputs, and uppercase/lowercase ordinal suffixes that tack on the right English ordinal (first, second, third, etc.) after any numeric value. These combine, so fill mode plus an ordinal suffix gives an unpadded number with the suffix attached. For text patterns like full month and day-of-week names the default output's always English no matter the JVM locale, and only when the translation modifier prefix is present should it reflect the JVM default locale. Full day-of-week names get padded to a consistent width with trailing spaces.
